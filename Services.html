03 · Step-by-step

How to see everything about your Gmail and Google account

Do this once to find out who has access, what is stored, and whether an attacker already planted a back door.

  1. Open "Last account activity" in Gmail. Scroll to the bottom-right of your inbox and click Details. You will see recent sign-ins with browser, location and IP. Click Sign out all other web sessions if anything looks wrong.
  2. Check forwarding. Gmail → Settings (gear) → See all settings → Forwarding and POP/IMAP. Attackers often add a hidden forwarding address to silently copy every email you receive. Remove any address you did not add.
  3. Check filters. Same settings → Filters and Blocked Addresses. Delete filters that auto-delete, archive or label messages from your bank or from Google security alerts.
  4. Check "Accounts" tab. Look at "Send mail as" and "Check mail from other accounts" for addresses you do not recognise.
  5. Open Security Checkup. Review devices, third-party access, recovery phone and recovery email. If your recovery details are old or not yours, change them first.
  6. Review third-party access. Remove apps and websites that can read your email or Drive if you do not use them anymore.
  7. Open My Activity. Use the filters (by date or product) to see what was saved and from which device. Delete sensitive items and turn on auto-delete.
  8. Download your data with Takeout. Pick the services you want (Mail, Contacts, Drive, Location History) to get a full copy. This shows how much personal information is stored. Keep the file private and delete it when done.
  9. Turn on 2-Step Verification and create a passkey. Then remove old "App passwords" you no longer use.

Found something you do not recognise? Change your Google password immediately from a trusted device, sign out of all sessions, then repeat the checks above. Also change the password of any account that uses the same email.

04 · Stay out of trouble

What to avoid, and what to do instead

Most online fraud works because someone was rushed into one of these mistakes.

🚫 Never

  • Share an OTP, PIN, CVV or MPIN with anyone, including "bank staff".
  • Install an .apk sent on WhatsApp, SMS or by a caller.
  • Install remote-control apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport) because a caller asks.
  • Tap links in SMS or WhatsApp that claim your account is blocked or you won a prize.
  • Pay a "processing fee", "tax" or "refundable deposit" to receive money.
  • Scan a QR code to receive money. Scanning is only for paying.
  • Send photos of your CNIC, card or a selfie holding your CNIC to strangers.
  • Use one password everywhere or save passwords in plain notes.
  • Do banking on free public Wi-Fi.
  • Trust caller ID. It can be faked.

✅ Do instead

  • Hang up and call the number printed on your card or inside the official app.
  • Install apps only from the Play Store or App Store and check the developer name.
  • Open your bank or wallet app yourself to verify any alert or payment request.
  • Use a different strong password for every site, stored in a password manager.
  • Turn on 2-Step Verification with an authenticator app or passkey.
  • Pause. Real emergencies survive a 5-minute verification call.
  • Tell a family member before sending money to anyone new.
  • Keep your phone, apps and browser updated.
  • Report and block scam numbers, then warn your contacts.

🚩 Red-flag phrases

"Your account will be blocked today""Don't tell anyone""Send a screenshot""Refundable deposit""Read me the code" "Install this to verify""You won a prize""Urgent: police case""Wrong transfer, please return""Limited seats, pay now"

07 · Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does CyberGuard see my Google or Gmail data?
No. The links above go straight to Google and other official sites. You sign in there, not here, and CyberGuard has no access to your accounts.
I shared my OTP with a scammer. What now?
  1. Call your bank using the number on your card and ask them to block the account or card.
  2. Change your banking, email and wallet passwords from a trusted device.
  3. Report it to the FIA Cyber Crime Wing and keep screenshots, numbers and transaction IDs.
  4. Warn family members who may be targeted next.
I clicked a scam link or installed a suspicious app.
  1. Turn on airplane mode and uninstall the app (Settings → Apps).
  2. Run Play Protect → Scan, then change passwords from another clean device.
  3. Check your bank and wallet apps for unknown activity and block cards if needed.
  4. If the phone still acts strangely, back up photos only and factory reset.
Why do you suggest an authenticator app or passkey instead of SMS codes?
SMS codes can be intercepted through SIM swapping or tricked out of you by a caller. Authenticator apps and passkeys stay on your device and are much harder to steal.
Is Google Play Protect enough on its own?
It is a good safety net and should stay on, but it cannot stop you from handing over your own OTP or installing an app you were tricked into allowing. Caution still matters most.
Are the CyberGuard tools a guarantee that something is safe?
No. A clean result means we found no warning signs, not that a link, file or person is guaranteed safe. Use the tools as one input and verify through official channels.
